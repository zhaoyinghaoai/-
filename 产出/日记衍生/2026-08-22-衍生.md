# 2026-08-22 日记衍生

## 【英文材料】

### 1. 口语短文（约 230 词）

Something funny happened today. I was reading a novel on my phone, and in the story, the main character pulled a random reward from his system — you know, like a loot box. He had no idea what the thing was for. He just shrugged and tossed it into his backpack. "I'll figure it out when I need it."

And for some reason, that little moment hit me.

Because honestly, that's us. That's everybody. We spend years learning stuff — skills, knowledge, random bits here and there — and half the time we don't know why we're learning it. It just sits there in our backpack, taking up space, looking useless.

Until one day life throws something at you, and you reach into that backpack, and there it is. The thing you picked up ages ago. Exactly what you need.

So maybe the stuff I'm learning right now — the AI tools, the writing, all of it — maybe none of it looks connected today. Doesn't matter. You don't keep a backup card because you know when you'll play it. You keep it because you might.

Today's move might look like a wasted turn. Tomorrow it might be the card that wins the game.

So yeah. I'm going to keep learning. Keep stuffing things into the backpack. Future me can worry about when to use them.

# 2026-08-22 日记衍生（晚上补记）

## 【英文材料】

### 1. 口语短文（约 220 词）

Honestly, not much happened today. But I made myself sit down and write this entry anyway, because there's an old saying I grew up with: don't put off until tomorrow what you can do today.

Here's the thing about procrastination — nobody forces it on you. You train it into yourself, one little "I'll do it tomorrow" at a time. You spoil yourself, basically. And once you get comfortable doing today's stuff tomorrow, tomorrow never runs out. There's always another one.

So tonight I closed the loop. Diary done today, not tomorrow. It sounds small, but I think that's exactly how the habit flips — you finish today's thing today, once, and it gets easier the next time.

Tomorrow morning I've got real work lined up: an article I've been meaning to write, plus a box of reading cards I bought forever ago and still haven't finished. Instead of saying "I'll get to them someday," I put them on tomorrow's list. Tomorrow already has its own job. It doesn't need yesterday's leftovers.

That's it for today. Nothing fancy happened. I just didn't leave anything behind — and weirdly, that feels like enough.
